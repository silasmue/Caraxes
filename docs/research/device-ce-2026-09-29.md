# The software CE's data movement on a device-owned host CE (design, 2026-09-29)

## What the software executor still does on Windows (run P32, 16 min)

- **The KMD's paging channel** (chid 15, PRIVILEGED, system VA space):
  - 925 VA-mode memsets, 19.7 GiB in total (allocation clears, up to 64 MiB each);
  - 1804 slices of 40 ms: **75 s of CPU**, about 250 MB/s through the BAR1 windows;
  - 29k physical-mode ops, 15 MiB (page-table writes and small copies).
- **UVMLite channels** (chid 34-51, GP100_UVM_SW): 4 KiB physical copies, about 250 MiB.
- The PyTorch first steps (17-121 s) and allocation-heavy paths wait on the memsets.

## Why not split the paging channel

- Its push interleaves physical page-table writes, VA-mode fills that go through those
  tables, and semaphores. Moving the fills to a host channel breaks the order inside the
  channel, or needs a cross-channel fence per method.
- A host channel for them needs the KMD's system VA space shadowed on the host, and
  privilege for the physical ops that remain.

## Proposal: keep executing the push, move the bytes

- The device keeps decoding the paging channel's push in order, with the validation it has
  now (`ce_pieces`: every piece lies in the VM's reservation or its registered guest RAM).
- The data movement of a large transfer goes to a **device-owned host CE**:
  - the invalidator's channel pattern (invalidate.rs): a COPY TSG on the device's own host
    client, an externally-owned VA space whose page tables the device writes in the
    reservation's carve-out;
  - that VA space maps **exactly the VM's memory**: the VRAM reservation at `D + host PA`
    (2 MiB PTEs, VIDEO) and each registered guest RAM chunk at `S + GPA` (sysmem PTEs with
    the chunk's IOVA);
  - each validated piece becomes a VA-mode copy or constant-remap fill on that channel,
    then a semaphore release; the device waits for it, then publishes the guest's
    semaphore as now.
- No guest method reaches the host, no privilege, and the channel can touch nothing
  outside the VM by construction (the VA space maps nothing else).
- **Small transfers stay on the CPU** (under 64 KiB per piece: page tables, semaphores), so
  the FB store's watches and walk caches stay exact. After a device-CE write to VRAM the
  device drops its walk caches, watched dwords and BAR TLB entries in that range.
- The 40 ms slicing stays as the CPU fallback: no device CE, a device-CE error, or a
  zero-based layout the mapping does not cover yet.

## Numbers and cost

- Expected: 19.7 GiB of fills in well under a second instead of 75 s; a 64 MiB fill in
  about 0.2 ms of GPU time.
- Page tables: 4 GiB VRAM = 8 PD0 pages, 8 GiB guest RAM = 16 PD0 pages, plus PD1/PD2:
  about 30 pages (120 KiB) in the 2 MiB carve-out, below the invalidator's 256 KiB.
- Linux gets the same path: its scrub memsets run through the same executor.

## Risks

- A bad PTE faults the device's own channel (a contained Xid on it): the device falls
  back to the CPU path for good and reports once.
- WC writes through the BAR1 windows before a device-CE push: flushed first, as the
  invalidator does.
- The UVMLite 4 KiB copies are too small per piece; batching several pieces into one
  device-CE push comes after.

Depends on: R (the reservation and its carve-out), the DMA table (registered IOVAs), K (PTE
kind generic, COMPTAGLINE 0).

## Implemented (2026-09-29, devce.rs)

- `DeviceCe`: the private pages `[E - 512 KiB, E - 256 KiB)` below the invalidator's; the guest's
  usable FB mapped at device VA 4 GiB with 2 MiB PTEs; a user-level channel on COPY2.
- The software CE (channel.rs `launch_dma`) plans a transfer of at least 64 KiB whose pieces are
  all plain VRAM in the window (`plan_fill`, `plan_copy`); otherwise, or on any refusal, it
  stays on the CPU with its 40 ms slices.
- One push at a time; the channel waits through the held replay (`ce-slices` clock), and the
  guest's semaphore follows the device CE's. After the push the FB store drops its caches for the
  written ranges (`FakeFb::note_range_changed`).
- A push not done in 2 s turns the engine off for good; the transfer is redone on the CPU, the
  guest channel is never RC'd for it.
- The device's private top 512 KiB is no guest memory any more: the software CE's range and the
  shadow's leaves stop below it.
- Default on, no switch. Built at the first doorbell; a refused setup leaves one log line.
